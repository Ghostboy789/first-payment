Exposure uses original principal as a proxy for the balance at risk. Loss given default is the share of that exposure the lender fails to recover. The scenarios hold both fixed across arms. Outstanding debt in the data rose by ₹148.7 per loan (95% CI ₹−16.4 to 313.8, n=845). That balance can still be recovered.

Break-even equals the lender’s additional contribution divided by exposure times loss given default. Both illustrative thresholds fall below the trial’s upper default bound.

The borrower profit gain was ₹906.6 per week (95% CI ₹168.7 to 1,644.4, n=752). Using that one week as a hypothetical budget implies 22.66 pp (95% CI 4.22 to 41.11 pp). The borrower receives that profit around three years after disbursement. The lender bears the credit loss on the trial loan. This comparison shows scale across horizons.

The surcharge covers only the modeled credit-loss difference. Funding, timing, operating costs and borrower responses to pricing are outside the calculation.
