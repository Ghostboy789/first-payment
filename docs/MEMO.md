# Credit committee memo

**Decision. Do not approve the grace-period pilot under the preset rule.**

The 2007 Kolkata trial compared the regular loan with a contract that began repayment after
two months. The new contract also extended maturity and reduced the effective interest rate.
Evidence comes from borrowers of one lender. [Trial paper](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.103.6.2196)

| Preset gate | What the trial found | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weekly profit lower 95% bound above ₹0 | ₹906.6 more per week among borrowers with recorded profit (95% CI ₹168.7 to ₹1,644.4, n=752) | PASS |
| Extra default upper 95% bound at most 2.00 percentage points | 6.14 points higher at 52 weeks past due (95% CI 1.20 to 11.09, n=845) | FAIL |

The 2-point cap is an illustrative risk budget fixed in the analysis plan. Default means full
repayment was still outstanding 52 weeks after the scheduled final instalment. Weekly profit
was self-reported around three years after disbursement. Removing the most extreme profit
values under the preset trimming rule makes the profit gate fail as well. The 93 missing
profit records leave room for materially different full-cohort conclusions.

**Credit cost.** On ₹8,000 of assumed exposure with 50% loss severity, the extra expected
credit loss is ₹245.6 per loan (95% CI ₹47.8 to ₹443.5). An illustrative ₹200 of additional
lender contribution per loan offsets a 5-point default increase at these assumptions. The
borrower profit estimate does not count as lender contribution. The surcharge excludes
funding, timing, operating costs and responses to a different price.

**What would change the decision.** At the same profit floor, the evidence would clear only
at a cap of at least 11.09 points on the site's hundredth-point slider. A lender holding the
2-point cap would need evidence that another contract or pilot cohort can meet that risk budget.
A pricing decision would also need measured exposure at default, recoveries and contribution
after financing and servicing costs.
